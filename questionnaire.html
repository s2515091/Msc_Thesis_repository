Data ecosystem questionnaire

This questionnaire helps you decide how to manage a data ecosystem project: a project in which data passes between actors, whether units of your own organization or separate organizations, and in which the conditions of that exchange still have to be settled, either for the first time or because an existing arrangement is under review. From your answers it ranks four management approaches for the project and explains the ranking.

It is meant for the enterprise architect involved in the project, answering alone or with a few colleagues in one meeting. No knowledge of the management approaches or the names they go by is needed: every question asks about your own project, and every answer comes with an example. Answer for this specific project rather than for your organization as a whole.

There are 23 questions in five sections. Section 3 asks about your organization, or about the organizations taking part, depending on your answer to Q7, and is skipped if Q7 is not decided yet. Answer for the project as it has been agreed today: what is already running, and what has been agreed but not yet built. Do not answer for ideas that have not been agreed; choose “I am not sure / not decided yet” instead. The method still returns a result and tells you which open questions would settle it. Your answers are not sent anywhere; the result is worked out in this browser and appears on this page once every question has an answer.

Example 1: a regional distribution hub

Fill in the answers given for a logistics project, to see how the questions and the result work before answering for a project of your own.

Read the project

A logistics service provider operates a regional distribution hub. Six independent subcontracted carriers deliver from it, each working for several other clients as well and each running its own transport management system: two of the six send files overnight, the others expose an interface of their own design. The hub intends to send each carrier its load plan and shipment status events during the day, and to receive vehicle positions and delivery confirmations in return, so that its planning system can reassign loads while the day is in progress. The planning platform is the hub’s own and has been in operation for several years. The carriers’ data is copied into it and used for other planning purposes as well, among them capacity forecasting and the annual carrier review. The hub sets the retention period, decides which of its own departments may see the data, and administers access to it; each carrier has signed a clause in its haulage contract that permits this. In return the carriers receive their load plans earlier and wait less at the dock, and no money changes hands. The project connects systems that already exist and creates no new roles or decision-making bodies, although the hub and the carriers have not yet agreed who carries the cost when a position is wrong or arrives late.

This questionnaire is the first component of a decision support method developed in a master’s thesis at the University of Twente. Its questions are drawn from published taxonomies of data sharing practices, data ecosystems and incentive mechanisms, a data governance framework, and a contingency approach to data governance.

      How the result is worked out. The four approaches are the corners of a matrix taken from Gieß and Hutterer (2025), with its two axes, application area and governance mode, as reconstructed in the thesis from an organizational perspective; the descriptions are the thesis’s own. Q7 settles the application area on its own. The governance mode is read from Q21 and Q22 first: their average, below zero for one party deciding for all and above zero for each party deciding for its own data. Only where that average is zero, or both are unanswered, do Q10 to Q13 decide, following the contingency approach to data governance of Weber et al. (2009). The approaches are then ranked: the project’s own approach first, then the one with the same application area and the other way of deciding, then the one in the other application area with the same way of deciding, and last the one that matches neither.
